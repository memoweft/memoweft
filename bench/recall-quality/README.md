# Synthetic paraphrase recall evaluation

All facts, accounts and hosts are fictional. The runner creates temporary SQLite stores and exercises `QueryService.preview_recall`, including permissions, currentness, correction notices and rendering. No real memory store or local model is used.

Run the frozen regression corpus with `npm run bench:recall-quality`. The historical matcher is loaded from Git commit `233cc2f`; fetch history first in a shallow checkout. The CI step uses three warm repetitions per query. Each case is also checked for deterministic output and absence of World writes. Latency uses the median warm call per query, then the nearest-rank p50/p95 over queries. Raw per-call percentiles are reported separately. Hardware-specific stored timings are descriptive; the 20% gate compares interleaved baseline/current calls in the same process.

| Split            | Queries | Positive / pure negative | Role                                                                                |
| ---------------- | ------: | -----------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `dev.json`       |     360 |                 300 / 60 | Only tuning corpus; 12 categories, 30 queries each                                  |
| `challenge.json` |     120 |                 100 / 20 | Original exposed holdout, permanently retired from unbiased evaluation              |
| `holdout.json`   |     120 |                 100 / 20 | Fresh final holdout; generated and committed separately before its first evaluation |

The categories are synonym, hierarchy, bilingual, colloquial, abbreviation, typo/pinyin, indirect constraints, time, correction, nearby competitors, polysemy and lifecycle. Development has 10 factual seeds per category and three queries per seed; the other splits have five seeds and two queries per seed. Multiple queries from one seed are correlated, so the query count is not the independent sample size. Both final splits have the same category and positive/negative proportions as development. Every non-target item is forbidden, including otherwise active nearby distractors. Negative recall is reported both over all queries and over pure-negative queries.

`baseline.json` retains the original development and initial-holdout baseline (the historical key `validation` now denotes `challenge.json`). `validation-rejected.json` is the untouched failed first holdout result: Hit@5 62%, 5/120 queries with false recall, two baseline positives lost. Its historical `holdout` hash matches `challenge.json`, not the fresh holdout. The challenged split was already exposed when this task was resumed. Its bytes and the rejected result are preserved; no new rule or vocabulary was tuned from it during the resumed work.

The final holdout generator was prepared but not evaluated by the interrupted session. The resumed session removes the provisional file, freezes the general ambiguity rule using development only, regenerates the same untested final split, and commits it separately. There is one final evaluation invocation after that commit. Later CI runs are fixed regression checks, with no tuning from their results. No third holdout is created, regardless of the final score.

`accepted.json` records the final category floors and fixture hashes. The gate rejects a new forbidden item, a lost historical positive, lower accepted category Hit@1/3/5 or MRR, an increased accepted false-recall rate, a lifecycle leak, or an interleaved p95 increase over 20%. The corpus hashes prevent silent fixture replacement. `--split dev` is the development-only command; `--split all` is the delivery/regression command. `--padding` is for optional scale checks, never part of the CI default.

The free comparison arms (`normalization.json`, `multigrain.json`, `topics.json`) and cloud experiment (`cloud.json`) retain the interrupted session's observations; they are not fresh measurements of the final unique-match rule. The unguarded topic arm was rejected for false recall. `development-final.json` and `scale-development.json` are resumed-session measurements. The two rejected scale attempts are also retained. The final 1,000-padding check has p95 173.160 ms baseline versus 138.147 ms current (20.2% faster), on 12 fixed development queries. This small scale sample is not a production capacity claim. Query-local token reuse avoids repeatedly normalizing a claim across fallback variants; the cache is released on every invocation and never spans a revision, account or permission change. `cloud_experiment.py` is optional and never imported by the product or CI.

The vocabulary in `recall_terms.py` is a manually authored bounded heuristic, not a learned or comprehensive multilingual model. It contains 89 groups / 341 written terms, 10 directed category rules, 10 constraint rules, 8 incompatible-domain rules and 11 detail rules. Most terms and guards mirror the development corpus; additional common terms overlap the original initial-holdout generator. Therefore even the first holdout was not fully blinded at vocabulary-design time, and the near-perfect development score must not be described as general semantic recall. No vocabulary was changed in the resumed session. Unknown words keep lexical matching; unsupported paraphrases often miss. Ambiguous multiple-topic matches abstain and retain the literal candidates. A known domain exclusion can deliberately remove an unrelated literal hit. NFKC, a bounded traditional-character map and Latin tone normalization are retrieval-only and never rewrite Evidence or stored claims.

The product change is Python Hermes / DSH / Trust-preview recall. The TypeScript engine and graph-first `world/recall.py` remain separate implementations. Storage schema remains Python 21, TypeScript/shared 6 and Portable 4; no fields, indexes, model calls, dependencies or migrations are added. Existing stored topic labels and permission-checked aliases remain authoritative; the static vocabulary does not recreate denied labels.

MiMo query rewriting was evaluated on 24 fixed development cases (20 positive / 4 pure negative), using `mimo-v2.6-flash`: 35% Hit@5, 12.5% of queries with a forbidden item, 3.506 s median and 9.828 s p95 model latency. The whole experiment made 27 requests including three synthetic answer smoke calls: 2,014 input tokens, 729 output tokens and 128 cached input tokens. At the [official real-time pricing](https://mimo.mi.com/docs/pricing), checked on 2026-10-11 (CNY 1 / million uncached input tokens and CNY 2 / million output tokens), the uncached upper-bound estimate is CNY 0.003472; this is not an invoice. The indirect-food answer smoke did not use the allergy claim (2/3 answers visibly used the supplied memory). This is a seeded-memory answer smoke, not a full model-backed ingestion/formation E2E test.

Query rewriting sends query text to a cloud provider and adds a model call on each recall; rewriting into several alternatives also increases irrelevant matching. Embedding/vector retrieval was not run: no embedding endpoint/model was configured, and a generation credential does not specify an embedding service. Consequently there is no measured vector quality, latency or price; choosing such a service and sending permission-eligible memory text for embedding would require a separate evaluation. Neither second-tier option is included in product code.

Existing formation already accepts durable topic entities and aliases, but this package does not alter its prompt, storage fields or model usage. A measured model-token delta for writing new aliases was not run; permission-safe reuse of existing labels was retained and covered by the full Python tests. There is no backfill requirement or background migration estimate because no store mutation is added.

## Final outcome (2026-10-11)

The single final evaluation at commit `5657cee` is retained in `final-evaluation.json`. Development Hit@5 is 99.67%; the final holdout is 66.00%, a 33.67 percentage-point gap. The exposed challenge reaches 64.00% after the unique-match guard, while its original failed 62.00% observation remains untouched in `validation-rejected.json`. These are evidence of substantial development-vocabulary overfitting, not evidence of general multilingual semantic recall.

The final evaluation gate **failed**: `holdout-polysemy-04-1` (`cat eye gemstone怎么收藏？`) newly recalls the pet-cat claim; the baseline returned an unrelated stamp claim on the same query, so the aggregate false-query rate still improves even though the strict per-item gate fails. No rule or vocabulary is changed after observing this result. `accepted.json` records category floors for future regression detection; its status explicitly states that the current baseline per-query gate has failed. Recording those floors does not approve this candidate or waive the gate. CI therefore also reports the same failure. The delivery is a draft PR for review, with the failed guard identified, not a claim that all checks are green.

The default offline corpus is 600 queries. Final evaluation used seven repetitions for warm latency and determinism in one invocation; subsequent three-repetition CI runs are frozen regression checks only. The final runner result has zero forgotten/expired/cross-account permission leaks and no lost historical positives. The one new polysemy error must be addressed in a separate, explicitly authorized iteration with an independent evaluation plan; this package does not create a third holdout.

`negative-audit.json` checks all 100 pure-negative queries plus false-positive positive queries against the frozen labels. It finds zero protected-state leaks and zero lost baseline positives. Final seven-repetition evaluation took 22.24 s locally; the three-repetition CI-equivalent corpus run took 13.19 s (the exact observed duration is recorded in `ci-verification.json`).
